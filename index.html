Selected work

Providence Health & Services

Lamplighter

The problem. Marketing was running campaigns across email, SMS, direct mail, social, and paid search, and nobody could reliably say which campaign a given result came from. URLs were tagged by hand, inconsistently. Phone numbers used in campaigns were tracked in scattered places, or not at all. The analytics team could describe channel performance but not campaign performance, which is the level at which decisions actually get made.

The approach. Rather than buy a tool, I designed and built one in-house. Lamplighter generates UTM-tagged campaign URLs from a controlled set of inputs and logs the phone numbers used across every campaign and tactic, so tagging is consistent by construction instead of by discipline. I later extended it so the campaign automation team could build marketing emails directly from its tracked URLs, UTM parameters, and phone numbers, removing manual entry and keeping campaign assets in sync with what analytics expected to see.

The outcome. Granular, campaign-level attribution across 150+ campaigns spanning five channels. The analytics team could finally answer campaign-level questions, and a category of manual work disappeared from the automation team's process.

Independent project, built on personal time

Multi-agent AI automation system

The problem. A set of recurring research, monitoring, and reporting tasks in my own life were eating hours a week. Each one was individually small and individually tedious: check these sources, compare against what I already have, flag what is new, summarize it.

The approach. I designed and deployed twelve scheduled AI agents, each scoped to a specific job, running against defined criteria on a set cadence. The system aggregates from ten-plus external sites and platforms, reconciles results against a structured tracking system with automated duplicate detection, and delivers digest reports on schedule.

The outcome. The work runs without me. More importantly, building it taught me where agent-based automation is genuinely reliable and where it needs a human check, which is the question every product organization is currently trying to answer.

About

I am a product person who came up through analytics. That order matters. Before I owned a roadmap, I spent years being the person who had to answer whether a campaign worked, which meant living with the consequences of every measurement decision made upstream. It made me skeptical of platforms that look impressive in a demo and fall apart when someone asks a specific question.

How I work

I start with the question underneath the request. Stakeholders usually arrive with a solution already picked, and most of the value in the first conversation is figuring out what problem that solution was chosen for. From there it is ordinary product craft: discovery, requirements specific enough to build from, honest tradeoffs between what the business wants now and what the platform can carry for five years.

I care a lot about adoption. A platform capability that nobody uses is a cost, not a win, so I spend real time teaching the teams we support: analytics, marketing managers, campaign automation, program managers. Several of the projects I am proudest of succeeded because of the training that came after launch, not the launch itself.

I also build things myself. Not because a director needs to, but because staying close to the making keeps me honest about what I am asking other people to do.

What I am working on now

I have been building and operating AI systems on my own time, entirely outside my work at Providence, and it has changed how I think about product work. I run twelve scheduled agents that handle research, monitoring, and reporting workflows I used to do by hand, including multi-source aggregation across ten-plus platforms and automated reconciliation against a structured tracking system. I also built a full-stack training application that I use several times a week, which is the fastest way I know to learn what it feels like to own something end to end.

This site is part of the same habit, designed and built with AI assistance.
