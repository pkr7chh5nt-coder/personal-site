Providence Health & Services

Turning diagnosis codes into targetable patient segments

The problem. Healthcare campaigns were built on broad, imprecise targeting, with no way to reach patients based on clinical risk factors without licensing or building a way to translate raw ICD-10 diagnosis codes into usable audience segments.

The approach. Led research, proposal development, and vendor selection for a medical code grouper, weighing build-vs-license tradeoffs before licensing a solution through Healthgrades. Defined how diagnosis codes would be grouped into risk categories that marketing and analytics teams could actually target against, then mapped the resulting ICD-10 codes to roughly 8 service lines and 100 sub-service lines so marketing and leadership had aligned performance visibility at both levels.

The outcome. Enabled condition-based patient targeting that powered live healthcare campaigns and improved response rates at a lower cost per campaign, one of the clearest lines I can draw between a technical/vendor decision and a measurable marketing outcome.

Providence Health & Services

Building campaign-level attribution from scratch

The problem. No unified way to attribute performance across channels.

The approach. Built "Lamplighter," an in-house tool for UTM-tagged URLs and phone logging; led the switch to Invoca for call tracking with real-time Dynamic Number Insertion. Went a step further with incremental attribution reporting that measured marketing-driven results against a holdout control group, proving causal impact rather than crediting correlation, and corrected cross-channel attribution by modeling actual in-home direct mail delivery dates instead of send dates.

The outcome. Granular attribution across 150+ campaigns spanning email, SMS, direct mail, social, and paid search, lower cost per campaign, better spend decisions.

Providence Health & Services

Redesigning an intranet used by 100,000 caregivers

The problem. Providence and Swedish shared an aging SharePoint 2010 intranet, 2.7M sessions a month, no real product ownership, and a design that hadn't kept pace with how caregivers actually worked.

The approach. Owned product strategy for the redesign end to end, from requirements through implementation, and directly managed a team of three delivering it. Ran user research and usability testing to make sure design decisions were grounded in how people actually used the site, not assumptions about how they should.

The outcome. Shipped a full redesign for a platform ~100,000 caregivers relied on daily, one of the largest single audiences of any project I've owned.

About

I've spent 13+ years in healthcare marketing technology, most recently as Director of MarTech Product at Providence, where I owned enterprise data and marketing platforms end to end: setting the vision, partnering with engineering and analytics, and turning messy business needs into products people actually use.

What I'm good at isn't glamorous. It's the unglamorous middle part of product work. Getting a CDP from "board-approved idea" to "marketing team relies on this daily." Standing up a team's first sprint cadence when there's no process to inherit and no time to wait for one. I like the parts of the job most people skip past on their way to the roadmap slide.

Outside of Providence, I build things for the same reason I like this work: because I want to see if I can make something people actually use. That's shown up lately as a full-stack training app I built for my own workouts, and a small fleet of AI agents I run to automate research and reporting I used to do by hand.

I'm a Certified Scrum Product Owner, I care about clear metrics and honest tradeoffs, and I build teams that ship. Currently exploring what's next: details below.
